\section{The timing model and trigger rules}
\label{sec:setup}

Let a scalar margin $M(t)\in\mathbb R$ summarize the remaining feasibility of a
mission, with $M(t)\ge0$ defining the monitored feasible set and $M(t)=0$ its
boundary.  During the degradation phase of interest, a fault drives the margin
downward, and
\[
  t_0=\inf\{t: M(t)\le0\}
\]
denotes the expiry of the positive margin.  All infima are taken over the
monitored time domain with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$; for the timing analysis I
assume that $t_0$ is finite and attained.  Let $t_f$ be the physical deadline by
which a recovery must have taken effect.  I assume that the monitored margin is
conservative, so that $t_f\ge t_0$, and define the post-expiry slack
$\delta=t_f-t_0\ge0$.

A recovery triggered at $t_{\mathrm{trig}}$ requires duration $\tau_r>0$ and
succeeds if
\begin{equation}
  t_{\mathrm{trig}}+\tau_r\le t_f.
  \label{eq:success}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $t_{\mathrm{pnr}}=t_f-\tau_r$ is the latest safe trigger time, or
point of no return, for that recovery.

I compare two trigger rules.  A fixed-margin reactive rule fires at
\[
  t_{\mathrm{trig}}=\inf\{t: M(t)\le\rho\},
\]
where the pad $\rho\ge0$ has the units of $M$.  The predictive rule estimates the
degradation rate $\hat r(t)=-\widehat{\dot M}(t)$ and, when that estimate is
positive, fires when the projected time to expiry is at most a horizon
$T_h\ge0$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    t_{\mathrm{trig}}=\inf\bigl\{t:{}&M(t)\le0\ \lor\\
    &[\hat r(t)>0\ \land\ M(t)\le T_h\,\hat r(t)]\bigr\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:predictive}
\end{equation}
The condition $M(t)\le T_h\hat r(t)$ is the projected-time test
$M(t)/\hat r(t)\le T_h$ multiplied through by $\hat r(t)>0$.  An unavailable or
non-positive rate estimate therefore leaves only the unpadded condition
$M(t)\le0$.  In both simulators of \cref{sec:method}, the margin is a time
headway measured in seconds, so the pad $\rho$ is also in seconds and the
degradation rate is dimensionless.
