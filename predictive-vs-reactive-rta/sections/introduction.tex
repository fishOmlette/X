\section{Introduction}

Runtime assurance layers monitor a system during execution and intervene when a monitored property is at risk. Modular runtime assurance for robotics switches between an advanced and a safe controller~\cite{soter}, and predictive runtime verification, enforcement, and monitoring aim to detect violations before they occur~\cite{pinisetty_re,pinisetty_rv,sankar_prm,radics}. Prediction is attractive because the extra lead may permit a completion-preserving recovery instead of an emergency stop.

A reactive rule can also intervene early, however, simply by using a larger fixed margin. We ask: \emph{when does predicting loss of feasibility actually outperform reacting to it?} This draft sets up a scalar margin model, shows that the horizon-based predictive trigger we study is a reactive trigger whose margin scales with the estimated degradation rate, and states when an earlier trigger can change the outcome of a recovery. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes a two-robot simulator for testing these conditions against a tuned fixed margin.
