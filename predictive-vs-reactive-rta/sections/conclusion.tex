\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For the trigger studied here, a projected time to margin expiry is a
current-margin threshold whose pad scales with the estimated degradation rate.
Earlier action changes a recovery outcome only within a band set by recovery
duration and deadline slack, observation age consumes that band one for one,
and a predictive horizon wastes less lead than a fixed pad when recovery
requires a similar lead, but a different margin, across cases.  In the
seed-disjoint single-margin evaluation, the tuned fixed rule records $0/6750$
conflict collisions and the tuned predictive rule records $2/6750$, so their
successful-only times remain descriptive.  In the coordination evaluation, both
tuned rules succeed in all 1,620 genuine-conflict episodes, and prediction
shortens mean makespan by $2.779$\,s (paired 95\% interval $[2.659,2.902]$\,s)
while serializing far fewer clear paths.  That result is limited to an immutable
one-shot ordering and to held-out noise on a fixed grid.  In both studies, the
predictive rule is compared with a tuned fixed threshold, and failures are
examined before average completion time.
