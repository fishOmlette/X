\section{Conclusion}

For the trigger studied here, projected time to margin expiry is a current-margin threshold whose pad scales with the estimated degradation rate. Earlier action changes a recovery outcome only within a specific timing band. A structural advantage over one fixed pad across cases also requires rates that vary and can be estimated.

In the seed-disjoint single-margin evaluation, the tuned fixed rule records $0/6750$ conflict collisions and the tuned predictive rule records $2/6750$. Their successful-only times are therefore descriptive, with no inferential efficiency comparison. The coordination study gives a different result: both tuned rules succeed in $1620/1620$ genuine-conflict episodes, and prediction reduces mean successful makespan by $2.779$\,s (paired 95\% interval $[2.659,2.902]$\,s) while serializing far fewer clear paths. That result is limited to an immutable one-shot ordering and held-out noise on a fixed grid. In both studies, the predictive rule is compared with a tuned fixed threshold, and failures are considered before average completion time.
